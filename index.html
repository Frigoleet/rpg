Blood and Deceit

Quick Battle Framework

1) Core Loop

  • Both sides start with their hit points (HP) from the sheet.
  • Roll initiative once: each rolls 1d20 + Dexterity modifier; higher goes first.
  • Turns alternate; on your turn choose one action and one movement.
  • Combat ends when a side hits 0 HP or yields; narrate outcomes.

2) Actions

  • Attack: Roll 1d20 + attack bonus vs target Defense. On hit, deal weapon damage + bonus.
  • Defend: Raise Defense by +2 until your next turn; you cannot attack this turn.
  • Quick Move: Reposition or take cover; gain advantage on your next Defense roll.
  • Creative Action: Describe a stunt (trip, disarm, shove, distract). GM sets a simple roll (1d20 + relevant ability) and an effect if successful.
  • Use Item/Ability: Drink potion, trigger class feature, or use a carried item; costs your action.

3) Attack & Defense

  • Attack Bonus: Proficiency (default +2) + main ability (STR for melee, DEX for ranged) + any magic/gear.
  • Defense Score: 10 + DEX modifier + armor/shield bonus + situational cover.
  • Hit Check: If attack roll ≥ Defense, it hits. Natural 20 always hits and doubles the weapon dice.
  • Damage: Weapon dice + STR/DEX modifier + magic/gear. Creative actions can swap damage for effects.
  • Glancing Blow (optional): Miss by 1–2 deals half damage if both agree, to keep fights brisk.

4) Hit Points & Conditions

  • Track HP for both sides. At 0 HP the side is defeated; narrate KO/capture/retreat instead of death if preferred.
  • Second Wind (1/encounter): As a bonus after taking damage, roll 1d6 + level to heal yourself.
  • Injury on Big Hit: If a single hit deals 10+ damage, the target suffers a minor setback (disarmed, winded: -1 to next roll).

5) Experience & Rewards

  • After victory, award 50 XP per character level of the defeated foe (or split evenly among players).
  • Creative play: grant +10 XP for smart stunts, roleplay, or problem-solving during the fight.
  • Loot: Use the NPC’s loot list as a base; add one flavorful item tied to the scene.

6) Creative Hooks

  • Terrain tags: assign 1–2 features (e.g., "slick floor", "hanging lanterns") that players can exploit for advantage.
  • Environmental assists: kicking a table for cover, cutting a rope, tossing sand—call for a simple roll to apply status (prone, blinded, slowed).
  • Social pivots: mid-fight intimidation or bargain—opposed CHA check; on success, pause combat or gain a one-turn edge.
  • Tags for NPCs: give each NPC a quirk ("cowardly", "berserker", "calculating") to guide tactics and reactions.
